\begin{abstract}
The least area covered by $n$ permutation strips in the unit square is of order $1/\log n$, even when the top intervals slide freely as in Kaczynski's original question. For each nondecreasing angular weight we determine the sharp projection bound at every scale. The critical weight $1/(1+\log(1/r))$ incurs a factor of order $\log\log n$. For each weight, one compact set realizes the bounds at every scale. It has Hausdorff length one, uniform coordinate marginals and Ahlfors regular length measure.
\end{abstract}

Divide the bottom of the unit square into $n$ equal intervals. Join each to an interval of the same width on the top by a parallelogram. Their areas add to one. How small can their union be? In his letter of 8 October 1998, Kaczynski allowed the top intervals to slide independently and asked whether the least covered area $\beta_n$ is of order $1/\log n$. He reported a proof of the lower bound and recalled a possible upper bound of order $(\log\log n)^2/\log n$ \cite[pp.~27--29]{Letter}. In the permutation version the top intervals also partition their edge. Write $\alpha_n$ for its minimum. Kuklinski proved $\alpha_n\lesssim(\log n)^{-p}$ for some $p>0$ \cite[Conjecture~2 and Theorem~4]{Kuklinski}.

\begin{theorem}[Kaczynski's second conjecture]\label{thm:area}
For $n\ge2$, $\beta_n\asymp\alpha_n\asymp1/\log n$. In particular,
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}(\log n)\beta_n\ge2,\qquad
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}(\log n)\alpha_n\ge\pi^2/4 .
\]
\end{theorem}

Already $\beta_2=1/\sqrt2$ while $\alpha_2=3/4$. Indeed the separation of the left endpoints at height $t$ is $d(t)=1/2+vt$, and the covered length is $1/2+|d(t)|$. Put $c=1/\sqrt2$. Since $\int_0^c t\,dt=\int_c^1t\,dt$, we have $\int_0^1|d|\ge\int_0^c d-\int_c^1d=c-1/2$, with equality for $v=-c$ (top starts $1/2,1-c$). The two permutations have $v=0,-1$ and areas $1,3/4$.

A strip joining $I$ to $J$ has horizontal section $\pi_t(I\times J)$ at height $t$, where $\pi_t(x,y)=(1-t)x+ty$. A permutation $\sigma$ selects $n$ such squares in the endpoint plane. Their union $P_\sigma$ has full projections at $t=0,1$. How quickly can its projections shrink between these edges?

Write $p_\theta(x,y)=x\cos\theta+y\sin\theta$, $s_\theta=|\cos\theta|+|\sin\theta|$, and $\rho_\theta=\min(|\cos\theta|,|\sin\theta|)/s_\theta$. For compact $K$ put $K_\delta=K+[-\delta,\delta]^2$. Given a nondecreasing $b:(0,1/2]\to[0,1]$, define
\[
 \Fav(K)=\int_0^\pi |p_\theta K|\,d\theta,\qquad
 M_b(K)=\sup_{\rho_\theta>0} b(\rho_\theta)|p_\theta K|,\qquad
 I_b(\delta)=\int_\delta^{1/2} b(r)\,\frac{dr}{r}.
\]
Here $|\cdot|$ denotes Lebesgue length. Logarithms are natural.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:profile}
Among compact $K\subset[0,1]^2$ with at least one full coordinate projection, the optimal joint bound at scale $0<\delta<1/2$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
 \inf_K\bigl[\Fav(K_\delta)+M_b(K_\delta)\bigr]
 \asymp \frac{1+I_b(\delta)}{\log(1/\delta)} .
\end{equation}
One set $S=S_b$ attains the upper bound up to an absolute constant for all $\delta$, with $\Fav(S_\delta)\asymp1/\log(1/\delta)$. It has $\mathcal H^1(S)=1$, and $\mu=\mathcal H^1|_S$ has uniform coordinate marginals and satisfies $\mu(B(z,r))\asymp r$ for $z\in S$, $0<r\le\diam S$. On the equal grid,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gridlaw}
 \min_{\sigma\in S_n}\bigl[\Fav(P_\sigma)+M_b(P_\sigma)\bigr]
 \asymp \frac{1+I_b(1/n)}{\log n}\qquad(n\ge2).
\end{equation}
The upper bound in \eqref{eq:gridlaw} can likewise be attained with $\Fav(P_\sigma)\lesssim1/\log n$. All comparison constants are absolute.
\end{theorem}

The logarithmic rate holds uniformly with weight $b$ precisely when $\int_0^{1/2}b(r)\,dr/r<\infty$. For $b(r)=1/(1+\log(1/r))$, one has $I_b(1/n)=\log[(1+\log n)/(1+\log2)]$. In fact $M_b$ alone then has minimum of order $(\log\log n)/\log n$. The proof gives zero length for every oblique projection of $S$.

Mattila obtained the logarithmic lower bound from positive one-dimensional Hausdorff content \cite{Mattila,CSS}. Chang, Shmerkin and Suomala attained this order with Ahlfors regular random sets and proved a $(\log j)/j$ lower bound at level $j$ for nested fixed-radix patterns with one square per column at every level \cite[Theorem~A.1]{CSS}. For prescribed projection directions and individual projected measures see \cite{Visibility,CS}. Our subdivision ratios vary.

\section{What a crossing counts}\label{sec:cross}

A \emph{pattern} $P=\bigcup_i I_i\times J_i$ matches two interval partitions of $[0,1]$ with corresponding widths $w_i=|I_i|=|J_i|$. Write $\ell_P(t)=|\pi_t(P)|$, $R(x,y)=(x,1-y)$ and $h(P)=\sum_i w_i\log(1/w_i)$. Let $Z_P(x,t)$ count the strips through $(x,t)$.

\begin{lemma}[Crossing identity]\label{lem:entropy}
For $W(t)=1/[4t(1-t)]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy}
 \sum_{Q=P,RP}\int_0^1 W(t)\int_{\R}Z_Q(Z_Q-1)\,dx\,dt=h(P).
\end{equation}
If only the bottom intervals partition $[0,1]$, the contribution of $P$ with weight $1/(2t)$ is at most $h(P)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $f(a)=a+v_i$ translate $I_i$ onto $J_i$. Ignore interval endpoints. Two particles starting at $a<a'$ meet at an interior height exactly when $f(a)>f(a')$. Put $p=a'-a$, $q=f(a)-f(a')$ on this inversion set $\mathcal I_f$. The meeting time is $t=p/(p+q)$. On a given pair of strips the map from a meeting $(x,t)$ back to its starting points $(x-tv_i,x-tv_j)$ has absolute Jacobian $v_i-v_j=p+q$. Thus for every nonnegative weight $\psi$,
\[
 \frac12\int_0^1\!\psi(t)\int_{\R} Z_P(Z_P-1)\,dx\,dt
 =\int_{\mathcal I_f}\psi\!\left(\frac p{p+q}\right)
                 \frac{da\,da'}{p+q}.
\]
With $\psi(t)=1/t$, each crossing contributes $1/(a'-a)$. The integral of this kernel over all pairs in distinct bottom intervals is $h(P)$. Indeed its integral over $0<a<a'<L$, $a'-a>\eta$, equals $L\log(L/\eta)-L+\eta$ for $\eta<L$. Subtract the contributions with $L=w_i$ from the one with $L=1$ and let $\eta\downarrow0$. This proves the one-sided bound. For matched partitions each pair in distinct bottom intervals crosses in exactly one of $P,RP$, since reflection reverses the order of the top intervals. The two contributions therefore exhaust $h(P)$. View the strips from the top, reflecting both edges of the second pattern to restore a common starting partition. This gives the same identity with $1/(1-t)$. Averaging the two identities proves \eqref{eq:entropy}.
\end{proof}

At each height $\int Z_Q\,dx=1$, so $\int Z_Q^2\,dx\ge1/\ell_Q(t)$. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
 \sum_{Q=P,RP}\int_0^1 W(t)(\ell_Q(t)^{-1}-1)\,dt\le h(P).
\end{equation}
To read off area bounds, let $V=W$ for a permutation and $V(t)=1/(2t)$ for freely placed tops. In both cases $\int V(1/\ell-1)\le\log n$. Cauchy--Schwarz on any closed interval $J\subset(0,1)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cs}
 \int_J g\ell\,dt\ge
 \frac{\bigl(\int_J\sqrt{gV}\,dt\bigr)^2}{\log n+\int_J V\,dt}
 \qquad(g\ge0).
\end{equation}
Take $g=1$, multiply by $\log n$, let $n\to\infty$, and exhaust $(0,1)$ by such $J$. The two integrals of $\sqrt V$ are $\pi/2$ and $\sqrt2$, giving Theorem~\ref{thm:area}'s lower constants. Formula~\eqref{eq:cs} also gives a lower bound of order $1/\log n$ on every fixed interval of heights with nonempty interior.

On the equal grid, a crossing pair with unsigned index gaps $d,e$ has overlap area $1/[n(d+e)]$ by the same Jacobian. As $1/(d+e)\le(1/d+1/e)/4$ and each edge has $n-d$ pairs of gap $d$, the two second moments sum to at most $H_n+1$, where $H_n=\sum_{d=1}^n1/d$. Each moment is at least one, giving $\alpha_n\ge1/H_n$. For freely placed tops a pair of bottom gap $d$ has area at most $1/(nd)$: in grid units its overlap at height $t$ is $(1-|d-vt|)_+$. If $v\ge d$, change variables and use the unit integral of the tent. If $v<d$, the tent is bounded by $(1-d(1-t))_+$. Summation gives $\beta_n\ge1/(2H_n-1)$. These are finite tube-overlap estimates \cite{Cordoba}.

For the angular lower bounds write $t=|\sin\theta|/s_\theta$ and $\tau(t)=\min(t,1-t)=\rho_\theta$. In each quadrant $p_\theta$ corresponds, up to sign and translation, to $s_\theta\pi_t$ on $P$ or $RP$. Changing variables gives
\[
 \Fav(P)=\sum_{Q=P,RP}\int_0^1g(t)\ell_Q(t)\,dt,\qquad
 g(t)=[t^2+(1-t)^2]^{-3/2}.
\]
In particular $\Fav(P_\sigma)\ge4/(H_n+1)$ by the two second moments. Applying the Cauchy--Schwarz argument on both copies of $J$ with their shared budget \eqref{eq:budget} gives the stronger asymptotic bound
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}(\log n)\min_\sigma\Fav(P_\sigma)
 \ge \left(2\int_0^1\sqrt{g(t)W(t)}\,dt\right)^2 .
\]
Also $b(t)\ell_{P_\sigma}(t)\le M_b(P_\sigma)$ for $0<t\le1/2$. Since $1/(4t)\le W(t)\le1/(2t)$ there, integration of \eqref{eq:budget} from $1/n$ to $1/2$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 I_b(1/n)\le 6(\log n)M_b(P_\sigma).
\end{equation}
Together these estimates prove the lower half of \eqref{eq:gridlaw}.

The compact lower bound needs only one visible axis. Suppose the horizontal projection of $K$ has length $m>0$ and take $n=\lceil1/\delta\rceil$. In each occupied column of width $1/n$, select a square of that side meeting $K$, with its horizontal interval equal to the column. Their union lies in $K_\delta$. The selected strips have total width $m_n\ge m$ at every height. If $d=nm_n$ columns are occupied, closing the gaps between them can only increase the bottom pair integrals. For $d$ adjacent intervals their sum is $m_n\log d$, by scaling the entropy calculation. For the covered length $\ell$ this gives
\[
 \int_0^1\!\left(\frac{m_n}{\ell(t)}-1\right)\frac{dt}{2t}\le\log d .
\]
On $[\delta,1/2]$ use $b(t)\ell(t)\le M_b(K_\delta)$ to obtain $m I_b(\delta)\lesssim M_b(K_\delta)\log n$. On $[1/4,1/2]$, Cauchy--Schwarz as in \eqref{eq:cs} gives $m\lesssim\Fav(K_\delta)\log n$. Transposition handles a visible vertical axis. This proves the lower half of \eqref{eq:law}, with a factor $m$ for partial visibility.

For the converse transfer, if $K$ carries a probability $\nu$ with uniform marginals, $n\nu(I_i\times I_j)$ has unit row and column sums for the half-open $n$-grid (close the last interval at $1$). Any $k$ rows meet at least $k$ positive columns, so Hall's theorem \cite{Hall} selects a permutation of positive cells. Their closures lie in $K_{1/n}$. Taking $n=\lceil1/\delta\rceil$ also transfers the asymptotic Favard lower constant from the grid to every such $K$.

\section{A shuffle that saves area}

If the squares of $P$ are $v_i+w_i[0,1]^2$, write $P\star Q=\bigcup_i(v_i+w_iQ)$. For any linear projection $p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:insert}
 |p(P\star Q)|\le\min(|pP|,|pQ|).
\end{equation}
Use containment and $\sum_i w_i=1$, respectively.

Partition $[0,u]$, $u\ge4$, into $m=\lfloor u\rfloor$ pieces, the first $m-1$ of length one. Make two copies, red and blue. On two edges of length $2u$ arrange them in the orders
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \text{bottom}&R_1,B_1,R_2,B_2,\ldots,R_m,B_m,\\
 \text{top}&B_1,\ldots,B_m,R_1,\ldots,R_m .
 \end{array}
\]
Match equal labels and rescale to the unit square to obtain $D_u$. Insert $P$ in each square. At height $t$, write $F=\pi_t(P)$. Within each color the two edge orders agree, so the projected copies have disjoint interiors and total length $|F|/2$. Their overlap is the saving (Figure~\ref{fig:shuffle}).

Pair $R_i$ with $B_{i+h}$, $1\le i\le m-h-1$. In units of their common width, blue starts $2h+1$ to the right at the bottom and $u-h$ to the left at the top. The displacement is $d_h=(2-t)h+1-(u+1)t$. The red members are disjoint in projection up to endpoints, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:save}
 |F|-|\pi_t(D_u\star P)|\ge
 \frac{m-h-1}{2u}\,|F\cap(F+d_h)|.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:scale}
If $0<t\le1/2$, $U\ge16$ and $Ut\ge8$, the saving averaged over $U\le u\le2U$ is at least $|F|^2/64$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The shift is $-s$, $0\le s\le1$, when $u=((2-t)h+1+s)/t-1$. The whole sweep lies in $[U,2U]$ for the integers
\[
 \frac{(U+1)t-1}{2-t}\le h\le\frac{(2U+1)t-2}{2-t}.
\]
There are at least $(Ut-1)/(2-t)-1\ge Ut/4$ such positive integers. Their $u$-intervals are disjoint and on each $h\le u/3$, so the prefactor in \eqref{eq:save} is at least $1/8$. Each sweep has $du=ds/t$. Finally $\int_0^1|F\cap(F-s)|\,ds=|F|^2/2$ counts the ordered pairs of points in $F$ above the diagonal. Summing the sweeps with density $1/U$ gives the bound.
\end{proof}

For a direct proof of the finite upper bound, let $A=\int_0^1\ell_P$. Transposing if necessary, assume $\int_0^{1/2}\ell_P\ge A/2$. Put $r_j=4^{-j}/2$ and $J_j=[r_{j+1},r_j]$. Since $\sum_{j\ge0}(j+1)2^{-j}=4$, some $J_j$ has $\int_{J_j}\ell_P\ge A(j+1)2^{-j}/16$, hence $\int_{J_j}\ell_P^2\gtrsim A^2(j+1)^2$ by Cauchy--Schwarz. Integrate Lemma~\ref{lem:scale} with $U=8/r_{j+1}$ over $J_j$. Some shuffle reduces the area to $A'$ with $A-A'\gtrsim A^2(j+1)^2$ at logarithmic cost $O(j+1)$. Thus $1/A'-1/A\gtrsim j+1$ and $j+1\lesssim1/A$.

Stop the first time $A\le1/k$. Each reciprocal gain is bounded below by an absolute constant, so the process stops. The preceding gains telescope to $O(k)$, and the last step costs $O(k)$ as well. The resulting pattern has $N\le e^{Ck}$ squares. Lebesgue measure on the bottom edge, transported along the matched diagonals, gives uniform marginals. Hall's matching places an equal-grid pattern in the $1/n$-thickening, increasing the covered area by at most $2N/n$. Take $k=(\log n)/(2C)$ for large $n$. This gives $\beta_n\le\alpha_n\lesssim1/\log n$.

\section{From one scale to all scales}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:block}
Let $v:(0,1/2]\to[0,1]$ be nondecreasing with $\int_0^{1/2}v(r)\,dr/r\le1$, and set $w(r)=\max(v(r),\sqrt r)$. For each integer $k\ge2$, at most $40k^3$ paths of $k$ shuffles suffice to form a pattern $Q$ of $N(Q)$ squares with
\[
 \log N(Q)\le32k,\qquad \Fav(Q)+M_w(Q)\le C/k,\qquad \diam Q\le1+1/(40k^3).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Start with the unit square. At stage $0\le i<k$ insert the current pattern in $D_u$ or its transpose, with $U_i\le u\le2U_i$, where $c(r)=\lfloor kw(r)\rfloor$ and
\[
 Y_i=\int_0^{1/2}\mathbf1_{\{i<c(r)\}}\,\frac{dr}{r},\qquad U_i=16e^{Y_i}.
\]
Each $r$ is counted $c(r)$ times, so $\sum_iY_i\le k\int_0^{1/2}w(r)\,dr/r\le(1+\sqrt2)k$. When $i<c(r)$, monotonicity gives $Y_i\ge\log(1/(2r))$, hence $U_i r\ge8$. Each stage uses at most $4U_i$ squares, so every path has at most $B=e^{8k}$.

Fix finitely many $t_j\in(0,1)$ and put $c_j=c(\tau(t_j))$. For any $a_j\ge0$ we claim some $i$-stage pattern $P$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:test}
 \sum_j a_j\bigl(\ell_P(t_j)-e_{ij}\bigr)\le0,\qquad
 e_{ij}=\frac{128}{128+\min(i,c_j)} .
\end{equation}
At $i=0$ this is equality. Put $F_j(x)=x-\mathbf1_{\{i<c_j\}}x^2/128$. Choose $P$ by \eqref{eq:test} at stage $i$ with weights $a_jF'_j(e_{ij})\ge0$ and write $x_j=\ell_P(t_j)$. Transposing the outer shuffle exchanges $t_j$ and $1-t_j$ in the displacement. When $i<c_j$, one orientation saves at least $x_j^2/64$ on average by Lemma~\ref{lem:scale}, and both savings are nonnegative. Averaging the weighted sum thus gives one $D$ with $\sum_j a_j\ell_{D\star P}(t_j)\le\sum_j a_j F_j(x_j)$. By concavity, $F_j(x_j)\le F_j(e_{ij})+F'_j(e_{ij})(x_j-e_{ij})$. Since $F_j(e_{ij})\le e_{i+1,j}$, summing proves \eqref{eq:test} at $i+1$.

Take the $J=2n$ mesh entries $h/(2n),1-h/(2n)$, $1\le h\le n=\lceil kB\rceil$. Put $\eta=1/(2k)$. At each step let $S_j$ sum the previous excesses $d_j(P)=\ell_P(t_j)-e_{kj}$ and use \eqref{eq:test} with $a_j=e^{\eta S_j}$. For $\Phi=\sum_j a_j$, the new excesses satisfy $|d_j|\le1$, so $e^x\le1+x+x^2$ on $[-1,1]$ gives
\[
 \sum_j a_j e^{\eta d_j}\le (1+\eta^2)\Phi+\eta\sum_j a_jd_j\le(1+\eta^2)\Phi .
\]
After $M$ paths, $e^{\eta S_j}\le\Phi\le Je^{M\eta^2}$. Take $M=40k^3\ge4k^2\log J$, since $J\le4kB\le e^{10k}$. Then $S_j/M\le\eta+(\log J)/(M\eta)\le1/k$, so the mean length $H$ satisfies $H(t_j)\le e_{kj}+1/k$. Round $0<t<1$ towards $1/2$ to a mesh height $t_j$ within $1/(2n)$. Each path has at most $2B$ interval endpoints moving at speed at most one, so $H(t)\le H(t_j)+B/n\le e_{kj}+2/k$. Since $w(\tau(t))\le w(\tau(t_j))<(c_j+1)/k$ and $w\le1$, we get $w(\tau(t))H(t)\le130/k$.

In the $2M$-grid match column $h$ to row $(h+M)\bmod 2M$ and insert the $M$ patterns in order in each half. The result $V$ has $\ell_V\le H$ by subadditivity. The selected diagonals lie on $y-x=\pm1/2$ in the central diamond of diameter one. Each point is within $1/(2\sqrt2 M)$ of its diagonal, so $\diam V\le1+1/M$. For $Q=V\star RV$, \eqref{eq:insert} gives $|p_\theta Q|\le s_\theta H(t)$ in either quadrant, hence $M_w(Q)\lesssim1/k$. Integrating gives $\Fav(Q)\lesssim1/k$ since $w(r)\ge\sqrt r$ and $|d\theta/dt|=s_\theta^2\le2$. Finally $Q\subset V$ and $N(Q)\le(2MB)^2=(80k^3 e^{8k})^2\le e^{32k}$.
\end{proof}

For $k=2^j$, $j\ge1$, put $A_k=1+I_b(e^{-k})$. The weight $v_k=b\,\mathbf1_{[e^{-k},1/2]}/A_k$ has integral at most one against $dr/r$. For $r<e^{-k}$, monotonicity gives $(k-\log2)b(r)\le I_b(e^{-k})$. Lemma~\ref{lem:block} and boundedness of projections thus give a block $Q_j$ with
\[
 \log N(Q_j)\lesssim k,\quad \diam Q_j\le1+C/k,\quad
 \Fav(Q_j)\lesssim1/k,\quad M_b(Q_j)\lesssim A_k/k .
\]

Nest $T_0=[0,1]^2$, $T_j=T_{j-1}\star Q_j$, and put $S=\bigcap_j T_j$. Both coordinate projections are full by compactness of the nested fibers. At level $j$ the sides sum to one and none exceeds $2^{-j}$. Descendants of a square of side $d$ have diameter at most $d(1+C2^{-j-1})$, so $\mathcal H^1(S)\le1$. Projection gives equality. For either coordinate $p$, projection gives $\mathcal H^1(S\cap p^{-1}(E))\ge|E|$ for every Borel $E\subset[0,1]$. Equality of total masses gives the uniform marginal. Off the countable subdivision endpoints each nested fiber is a singleton.

For $k=2^j$, $\log N(T_j)\le C_1k$ by summing the costs. Thickening one square by $\delta$ adds $2s_\theta\delta$ to its projected length. Using \eqref{eq:insert} and summing over the squares gives
\[
 \Fav(S_\delta)\lesssim k^{-1}+\delta e^{C_1k},\qquad
 M_b(S_\delta)\lesssim A_k/k+\delta e^{C_1k}.
\]
For small $\delta$ choose the largest dyadic $k$ with $2\max(C_1,1)k\le\log(1/\delta)$. Then $A_k\le1+I_b(\delta)$ and the error is at most $\sqrt\delta$. Boundedness covers the remaining scales. The extra $\sqrt r$ bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:block} gives $|p_\theta S|=0$ for every $\rho_\theta>0$. In particular $S$ is purely unrectifiable by the Besicovitch projection theorem \cite[\S1.1]{CSS}.

Every elementary square of side $d$ has mass $d$ by its horizontal marginal. A parent of side $L$ has two ordered families of children, each of total side $L/2$, with sides in $[h,2h]$ and successive centers at distance at most $5h$. For a shuffle $h=L/(2u)$, with horizontal and vertical steps at most $4h$ and $2h$. For the half-shift $h=L/(2M)$ along the two diagonals. Reflection and transposition preserve these bounds.

For $z\in S$, $0<r\le\diam S$, follow a chain through $z$ to the first square of side at most $r/8$. Its parent has $L>r/8$ and $h\le r/8$. The child's family has at least $L/(4h)>r/(32h)$ members. Choose $1+\lfloor r/(32h)\rfloor$ consecutive members including this child. All lie in $B(z,r)$ since $5r/32+2\sqrt2h<r$. Their mass is at least $r/32$, and the horizontal marginal gives the upper bound $2r$.

Finally Hall's grid matching applied to $S$ at $\delta=1/n$ gives \eqref{eq:gridlaw} and the simultaneous Favard bound (enlarge constants for $n=2$). The leading constants and Kaczynski's monotonicity question remain open.
