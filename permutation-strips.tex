\begin{abstract}
Match the $n$ equal intervals on the bottom of the unit square to those on the top by a permutation. The least area of the union of the resulting parallelograms is of order $1/\log n$, as conjectured by Kaczynski. We prove the same order when the top intervals can slide independently, as in his original formulation. A weighted crossing identity gives lower bounds of $\pi^2/4$ and $2$, respectively, for the liminf of the minimum area times $\log n$. The same construction gives a compact $1$-Ahlfors regular set of Hausdorff length one whose length measure projects to Lebesgue measure on both coordinate axes. Its $\delta$-neighborhood has Favard length of order $1/\log(1/\delta)$, and its projection lengths are $O(1/\log(1/\delta))$ uniformly on each closed arc of directions disjoint from the axes. For nondecreasing angular weights, we give a necessary and sufficient integral condition for this rate near the axes.
\end{abstract}

Divide the bottom edge of the unit square into $n$ equal intervals. From each interval draw a parallelogram to an interval of the same width on the top edge. The areas add to one. How small can the area of their union be? In a letter of 8 October 1998, Kaczynski allowed each top interval to slide independently in $[0,1]$. He asked whether the minimum, denoted here by $\beta_n$, is of order $1/\log n$, and reported a proof of the lower bound. He also recalled a possible upper bound of order $(\log\log n)^2/\log n$ \cite[pp.~27--29]{Letter}. In Kuklinski's permutation formulation the top intervals also partition their edge. Write $\alpha_n$ for this minimum. Kuklinski proved $\alpha_n\le C(\log n)^{-p}$ for some $p>0$ \cite[Conjecture~2 and Theorem~4]{Kuklinski}. Unsubscripted logarithms are natural.

\begin{theorem}[Kaczynski's second conjecture]\label{thm:finite}
For every $n\ge2$,
\[
 \frac{c}{\log n}\le\beta_n\le\alpha_n\le\frac{C}{\log n}
\]
with absolute constants $c,C>0$. On the permutation grid $\alpha_n\ge1/H_n$, where $H_n=\sum_{j=1}^n1/j$. The asymptotic lower bounds in the two models satisfy
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}(\log n)\beta_n\ge2,\qquad
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}(\log n)\alpha_n\ge\frac{\pi^2}{4}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The two endpoints of a segment give a point $(x,y)$ in a second copy of the unit square. At height $t$ the segment has horizontal coordinate $\pi_t(x,y)=(1-t)x+ty$. Thus the strip joining $I$ to $J$ is encoded by the square $I\times J$, and its section at height $t$ is the interval $\pi_t(I\times J)$. This description also gives a compact version of the problem.

For the lower bound we follow a pair of strips through its crossing. A weight singular at the two edges turns the total overlap, summed over a matching and its reflection, into the entropy of the interval widths. For the upper bound we put translated copies of the same pattern into two ordered families of squares. At a chosen height the translation varies with the scale of the shuffle. Averaging that translation counts pairs of points in the section and gives a saving proportional to its squared length. Inserting independent realizations in separate squares lets us average their section lengths at every height simultaneously.

For compact $K\subset\R^2$, put $K_\delta=K+[-\delta,\delta]^2$ and
\[
  \Fav(K)=\int_0^\pi |p_\theta(K)|\,d\theta,
  \qquad p_\theta(x,y)=x\cos\theta+y\sin\theta.
\]
Here $|\cdot|$ denotes Lebesgue length and $\Fav$ is unnormalized. Write $p_x,p_y$ for the coordinate projections and $\mathcal L^1$ for Lebesgue measure on $[0,1]$. To describe directions near an axis, set
\[
 \tau(t)=\min(t,1-t),\qquad
 \rho_\theta=\frac{\min(|\cos\theta|,|\sin\theta|)}
 {|\cos\theta|+|\sin\theta|}.
\]
Thus $\rho_\theta=0$ on the axes and $\rho_\theta=1/2$ on the diagonals. A positive nondecreasing function $b$ on $(0,1/2]$ will specify a directional bound with angular factor $1/b(\rho_\theta)$. We normalize $b\le1$.

\needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:compact}
Suppose $b:(0,1/2]\to(0,1]$ is nondecreasing and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:criterion}
 \int_0^{1/2} b(r)\,\frac{dr}{r}<\infty.
\end{equation}
There is a compact set $S=S_b\subset[0,1]^2$ of Hausdorff length one such that, for $\mu=\mathcal H^1|_S$,
\[
 (p_x)_\#\mu=(p_y)_\#\mu=\mathcal L^1,\qquad
 \mu(B(z,r))\asymp r\quad(z\in S,\ 0<r\le\diam S).
\]
Moreover,
\[
  \Fav(S_\delta)\asymp\frac1{\log(1/\delta)}
  \qquad (0<\delta<\tfrac12).
\]
At every oblique angle, that same set satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:directional-thm}
 b(\rho_\theta)\,|p_\theta(S_\delta)|
 \le \frac{C_b}{\log(1/\delta)}
 \qquad(\rho_\theta>0,\ 0<\delta<1/2).
\end{equation}
Conversely, if a compact $K\subset[0,1]^2$ carrying a probability measure with uniform coordinate marginals satisfies \eqref{eq:directional-thm} in place of $S$, then $b$ satisfies \eqref{eq:criterion}.
\end{theorem}

For example, write $L(r)=1+\log(1/r)$. One may take $1/b(r)=L(r)[1+\log L(r)]^2$, whereas $1/b(r)=L(r)$ violates \eqref{eq:criterion}. Every closed arc away from the axes therefore has a uniform $O(1/\log(1/\delta))$ bound. In the limit, exactly the two coordinate projections have positive length. The constants in the projection estimates may depend on $b$.

Mattila's projection estimate gives the logarithmic lower bound for sets of positive one-dimensional Hausdorff content \cite{CSS,Mattila}. Chang, Shmerkin and Suomala constructed $1$-Ahlfors regular random Cantor sets with this sharp Favard decay \cite{CSS}. Cs\"ornyei and Suomala characterized the measures that can occur as one projection of a purely unrectifiable length measure \cite{CS}. Cs\"ornyei also showed that a compact set can have any prescribed closed set of positive-length projection directions \cite[Theorem~1]{Visibility}. In Theorem~\ref{thm:compact} each coordinate projection accounts for the entire length of $S$, and \eqref{eq:criterion} describes how the rate in the other directions can depend on angle. The choice of scales matters. On a fixed $L$-adic grid, nested patterns with one square per column at every level have Favard length at least $c_L(\log j)/j$ at level $j$ \cite[Theorem~A.1]{CSS}. Our construction varies the subdivision ratios, and the grid matchings are chosen separately for each $n$.

\section{Crossings on the equal grid}

For a compact set $K$ of endpoints, let $\A(K)=\int_0^1|\pi_t(K)|\,dt$. For a union of endpoint squares this is the area covered by the strips. Reflection $R(x,y)=(x,1-y)$ interchanges the two quadrants of projection directions. Indeed, setting $t=\sin\theta/(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:angle}
 \A(K)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{|p_\theta(K)|}{(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)^3}\,d\theta,
 \qquad
 \A(K)+\A(RK)\le\Fav(K)\le 2\sqrt2\,\bigl(\A(K)+\A(RK)\bigr).
\end{equation}

The second-moment argument is related to the pairwise tube-overlap estimate in the planar Kakeya problem \cite{Cordoba}. Here the order of the endpoints identifies every crossing explicitly. Let $P_\sigma$ be the union of the $n$ closed grid squares selected by a permutation $\sigma$. Let $Z$ count its strips through a point and put $E=\int Z^2$. Since $\int Z=1$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\A(P_\sigma)\ge1/E$. Only inversions contribute to the pairwise overlaps. If an inverted pair has index gaps $p$ and $q$ on the two edges, its intersection area is
\[
 \frac1n\int_0^1\bigl(1-|p-(p+q)t|\bigr)_+\,dt
 =\frac1{n(p+q)}\le\frac1{4n}\left(\frac1p+\frac1q\right).
\]
The integrand is a tent of height one and base $2/(p+q)$, supported in $[0,1]$.

Let $E'$ be the second moment for the reflected pattern $RP_\sigma$. Every pair $i<j$ crosses in exactly one of the two matchings, with the same unsigned gaps $p_{ij}=j-i$ and $q_{ij}=|\sigma(j)-\sigma(i)|$. On each edge precisely $n-d$ pairs have gap $d$. Counting each intersection twice gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:harmonic}
 E+E'=2+\frac2n\sum_{i<j}\frac1{p_{ij}+q_{ij}}
 \le 2+\frac1n\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}\frac{n-d}{d}=H_n+1.
\end{equation}
The diagonal terms alone give $E'\ge1$, so $E\le H_n$. This proves $\alpha_n\ge1/H_n$. Using both quadrants in \eqref{eq:angle} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:both}
 \Fav(P_\sigma)\ge\frac1E+\frac1{E'}\ge\frac4{E+E'}\ge\frac4{H_n+1}.
\end{equation}

For the reverse permutation $E=H_n$, since all pairs cross and $p=q$. Yet its section at height $t$ has length $n^{-1}+(1-n^{-1})|1-2t|$. Indeed the projected intervals have width $1/n$ and consecutive centers are $|1-2t|/n$ apart. Its area tends to $1/2$. The large second moment comes from crossings near $t=1/2$.

\begin{lemma}[Grid matching]\label{lem:hall}
If a compact $K\subset[0,1]^2$ carries a probability measure with uniform coordinate marginals, then for each $n\ge2$ some $P_\sigma$ lies in $K_{1/n}$. In particular,
\[
 \alpha_n\le\A(K_{1/n}),\qquad \Fav(K_{1/n})\ge\frac4{H_n+1}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use half-open grid intervals $I_i$, closing the last one at $1$. For the given measure $\nu$, the matrix $a_{ij}=n\nu(I_i\times I_j)$ has all row and column sums equal to one. Any $k$ rows therefore have positive entries in at least $k$ columns. Hall's theorem \cite{Hall} supplies a permutation of positive entries. Each selected cell meets $K$, so its closure lies in $K_{1/n}$. Apply \eqref{eq:both} and the definition of $\alpha_n$.
\end{proof}

\section{Weighted crossings and entropy}\label{sec:entropy}

Consider a matching $P$ between two partitions of $[0,1]$ by intervals
$I_i$ and $J_i$ of equal corresponding widths $w_i=|I_i|=|J_i|>0$.
Its endpoint squares are $I_i\times J_i$. Write
\[
 h(P)=\sum_i w_i\log(1/w_i),\qquad W(t)=\frac1{4t(1-t)} .
\]
For $Q=P,RP$, let $Z_Q(x,t)$ count the strips of $Q$ at $(x,t)$ and
let $\ell_Q(t)=|\pi_t(Q)|$. At each height, $\int Z_Q(x,t)\,dx=1$.
All sums indexed by $Q=P,RP$ count the two orientations with multiplicity.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted crossing identity]\label{lem:entropy}
For every such matching,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy}
 \sum_{Q=P,RP}\int_0^1 W(t)\int_{\R}Z_Q(x,t)(Z_Q(x,t)-1)\,dx\,dt
 =h(P).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reciprocal}
 \sum_{Q=P,RP}\int_0^1 W(t)\bigl(\ell_Q(t)^{-1}-1\bigr)\,dt\le h(P).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Order a pair by its bottom intervals. It crosses in exactly one orientation.
Let $p,q$ be the distances between its centers on the bottom and top,
and center intervals $U,V$ of the two widths at zero.
With $L(s)=|U\cap(s+V)|$, the overlap at height $t$ is $L(p-(p+q)t)$.
The support of the even function $L$ is contained in $[-q,p]$, since
$p,q\ge(|U|+|V|)/2$. Substitution $s=p-(p+q)t$ gives
\[
 \int_0^1\frac{L(p-(p+q)t)}{t}\,dt
 =\int_{-q}^p\frac{L(s)}{p-s}\,ds
 =\int_{I_i\times I_j}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|}.
\]
The last equality follows by integrating over $s=u-v$ with $u\in U$ and $v\in V$, then
placing their centers at distance $p$. Replacing $t$ by $1-t$ gives the
same formula for the top intervals at distance $q$.

For any partition into intervals $I_i$ of lengths $w_i$, its sum of these
integrals over unordered pairs equals $h(P)$. Indeed, the integral of
$1/(y-x)$ over $0\le x<y\le b$ with $y-x>\eta$ is
$b\log(b/\eta)-b+\eta$ for $0<\eta<b$. Subtract the within-interval
integrals from that for $b=1$ and let $\eta\downarrow0$.
Now $4W(t)=1/t+1/(1-t)$, while $Z_Q(Z_Q-1)$ counts each crossing twice.
The bottom and top partitions each contribute $h(P)$, proving
\eqref{eq:entropy}. Slice Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$\int Z_Q^2\,dx\ge1/\ell_Q(t)$ and hence \eqref{eq:reciprocal}.
\end{proof}

For Kaczynski's original problem the top intervals can overlap. The bottom half of the identity still gives a bound. Order two strips by their bottom centers, at distance $p$, and let $b$ be the signed difference of their top centers in the same order. If $b\ge p$, their overlap has zero area. Otherwise the same substitution, now $s=p-(p-b)t$, gives
\[
 \int_0^1 L(p-(p-b)t)\,\frac{dt}{t}
   =\int_b^p \frac{L(s)}{p-s}\,ds
   \le\int_{I_i\times I_j}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|}.
\]
A crossing may stop partway through the support of $L$. This explains why the equality becomes an inequality. Summing over the bottom partition gives $\int_0^1(1/\ell(t)-1)\,dt/(2t)\le\log n$, where $\ell$ is the length covered at height $t$. Cauchy--Schwarz on $[\varepsilon,1]$ now gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:free}
 \beta_n\ge
 \frac{2(1-\sqrt\varepsilon)^2}{\log n+\tfrac12\log(1/\varepsilon)}
 \qquad(0<\varepsilon<1).
\end{equation}
Taking $\varepsilon=(\log n)^{-2}$ for large $n$ proves the lower bound for $\liminf (\log n)\beta_n$. Since $\beta_n>0$ for each $n$ by \eqref{eq:free}, decreasing $c$ covers all $n\ge2$.

To extract the permutation bound, choose one or both orientations, writing $m=1$
or $2$ for their number. For any continuous $g>0$ on $[0,1]$, truncate to
$J=[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]$, $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. Cauchy--Schwarz
on these $m$ copies of $J$, followed by \eqref{eq:reciprocal}, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-CS}
 \sum_{Q}\int_0^1g(t)\ell_Q(t)\,dt
 \ \ge\ \frac{\bigl(m\int_J\sqrt{g(t)W(t)}\,dt\bigr)^2}
 {h(P)+m\int_J W(t)\,dt}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the two squared factors in Cauchy--Schwarz are $g\ell_Q$ and
$W/\ell_Q$, and $\ell_Q\le1$. On the equal grid $h(P)=\log n$.
For $g=1$ and $m=1$ this yields the finite estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-finite}
 \alpha_n\ge
 \frac{(\pi/2-2\arcsin\sqrt\varepsilon)^2}
 {\log n+\tfrac12\log((1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon)}.
\end{equation}
Taking $\varepsilon=(\log n)^{-2}$ for sufficiently large $n$ gives
$\liminf_{n\to\infty}(\log n)\alpha_n\ge\pi^2/4$.

The same argument restricted to a fixed interval $J\subset(0,1)$ of positive length gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window}
 \int_J\ell_{P_\sigma}(t)\,dt\ge
 \frac{\bigl(\int_J\sqrt{W(t)}\,dt\bigr)^2}{\log n+\int_J W(t)\,dt}
 \qquad(\overline J\subset(0,1)).
\end{equation}
In particular the order $1/\log n$ cannot be improved uniformly on any such interval. The same identity proves the necessity of \eqref{eq:criterion}. Suppose there are permutations at arbitrarily large $n$ with $b(\tau(t))\ell_{P_{\sigma_n}}(t)\le C_b/\log n$ for every $0<t<1$. Divide \eqref{eq:reciprocal} by $\log n$ and keep one orientation. The integrand is nonnegative, so Fatou's lemma gives
\[
 \int_0^1 W(t)b(\tau(t))\,dt\le C_b .
\]
Since $4W(t)\ge1/t$, the integral in \eqref{eq:criterion} is finite. If a compact $K$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:compact} satisfies \eqref{eq:directional-thm}, Lemma~\ref{lem:hall} selects such permutations at $\delta=1/n$. Indeed $\pi_t=p_\theta/(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)$ in the first quadrant, with $\tau(t)=\rho_\theta$.

For both orientations the change of angle in \eqref{eq:angle}
gives $\Fav(P)=\sum_{Q=P,RP}\int_0^1 g(t)\ell_Q(t)\,dt$ with
$g(t)=(t^2+(1-t)^2)^{-3/2}$. Apply \eqref{eq:weighted-CS} with
$m=2$ and the same cutoff. Uniformly over equal-grid permutations,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fav-constant}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}(\log n)\min_{\sigma\in S_n}\Fav(P_\sigma)
 \ \ge\ c_{\rm F},\qquad
 c_{\rm F}=\left(\int_0^1
 \frac{dt}{\sqrt{t(1-t)}\,[t^2+(1-t)^2]^{3/4}}\right)^2 .
\end{equation}

Lemma~\ref{lem:hall} transfers this estimate to every compact $K\subset[0,1]^2$ carrying a probability measure with uniform coordinate marginals. Indeed, taking $n=\lceil1/\delta\rceil$ gives $P_\sigma\subset K_\delta$ and $\log n\sim\log(1/\delta)$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fav-continuous}
 \liminf_{\delta\downarrow0}\log(1/\delta)\,\Fav(K_\delta)\ge c_{\rm F}.
\end{equation}

\section{The shuffle}

A \emph{pattern} is a finite union $P=\bigcup_i(I_i\times J_i)$ in which each family of intervals partitions $[0,1]$ up to endpoints and $|I_i|=|J_i|=w_i>0$. Write $N(P)$ for the number of squares. If $P=\bigcup_i(v_i+w_i[0,1]^2)$, insert a copy of $Q$ in each square by setting $P\star Q=\bigcup_i(v_i+w_iQ)$. For any linear projection $\pi$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:insert}
 |\pi(P\star Q)|\le\min\{|\pi(P)|,|\pi(Q)|\}.
\end{equation}
Containment gives the first bound. The second follows by summing the copy ratios $w_i$. The widths of the new pattern are products of the two sets of widths, so $h(P\star Q)=h(P)+h(Q)$.

Fix a real $u\ge4$. Partition $[0,u]$ into $m=\lfloor u\rfloor$ pieces, the first $m-1$ of length one and the last of length $1+u-m$. Make a red and a blue copy of the partition. On two edges of length $2u$, arrange the pieces in the orders
\[
 \begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
 \text{bottom}&R_1,B_1,R_2,B_2,\ldots,R_m,B_m,\\
 \text{top}&B_1,B_2,\ldots,B_m,R_1,R_2,\ldots,R_m .
 \end{array}
\]
Match equal labels and rescale by $1/(2u)$ to obtain $D_u$. Its sides lie in $[1/(2u),1/u]$. Put $P'=D_u\star P$, $F_t=\pi_t(P)$ and $\ell(t)=|F_t|$. Within each color the squares occur in the same order on both axes. Their projections have disjoint interiors and total covered length $\ell(t)/2$ after insertion.

Choose an offset $h\ge1$ and pair $R_i$ with $B_{i+h}$ for $1\le i\le m-h-1$. All these squares have the same width. In units of that width, blue starts $2h+1$ units to the right of red on the bottom edge and $u-h$ units to its left on the top edge. Its displacement at height $t$ is
\[
 d_{u,h}(t)=(2h+1)(1-t)+(h-u)t=(2-t)h+1-(u+1)t.
\]
The paired intersections have disjoint interiors since their red members do. After rescaling by $1/(2u)$ their sum gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:save}
 \ell(t)-|\pi_t(P')|\ge\frac{m-h-1}{2u}
 |F_t\cap(F_t+d_{u,h}(t))|\qquad(1\le h\le m-2).
\end{equation}
For $u=4$, $h=1$, and $t=1/2$, the displacement vanishes. Figure~\ref{fig:shuffle} shows why the same pairing works even if $F_t$ has many gaps.

At a small height $t$, a crossing requires a scale $u$ of order $1/t$ or larger. As $u$ varies beyond that scale, the section of a red copy sweeps across successive blue copies. The following estimate uses a different offset $h$ each time another blue copy comes into range (Figure~\ref{fig:sweeps}).

\begin{lemma}[Averaging the shuffle scale]\label{lem:shell}
If $U\ge16$, $0<t\le1/2$ and $Ut\ge 8$, then $u$ uniform on $[U,2U]$
gives $\mathbb E_u[\ell(t)-|\pi_t(D_u\star P)|]\ge\ell(t)^2/64$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $0\le s\le1$, the choice
\[
 u_h(s)=\frac{(2-t)h+1+s}{t}-1
\]
gives displacement $-s$. We have $u_h([0,1])\subset[U,2U]$ whenever
\[
 \frac{(U+1)t-1}{2-t}\le h\le \frac{(2U+1)t-2}{2-t}.
\]
The lower endpoint exceeds one. The number of integers in the
interval is at least $(Ut-1)/(2-t)-1\ge Ut/4$, since $Ut\ge8$.
Their parameter intervals $u_h([0,1])$ are disjoint, because the starts
are $(2-t)/t$ apart and the interval length is $1/t$. For $u=u_h(s)$ we
have $h\le u/3$, as $t\le1/2$. Thus
$(\lfloor u\rfloor-h-1)/(2u)\ge(2u/3-2)/(2u)\ge1/8$.
On each such interval $du=ds/t$. Finally
$\int_0^1|F_t\cap(F_t-s)|\,ds=|F_t|^2/2$ by Fubini, counting the half of
$F_t\times F_t$ above the diagonal. Integration with density $1/U$ and
summation over the selected integers give the claim.
\end{proof}

Fix a nondecreasing $b:(0,1/2]\to(0,1]$ satisfying \eqref{eq:criterion}. To prove Theorem~\ref{thm:finite} one may take $b(r)=r$. Constants below may depend on the fixed $b$. Put $r_0=1$ and, for $j\ge1$, set
\[
 r_j=\max\{b(\min(1/2,2^{1-j})),(1+j)^{-2}\}.
\]
The sequence decreases to zero and is summable. Indeed $\int_{2^{1-j}}^{2^{2-j}} b(r)\,dr/r\ge(\log 2)b(2^{1-j})$ for $j\ge3$. Take a nonnegative integer $J$ with $\mathbb P(J\ge j)=r_j$, set $U=16\cdot2^J$, and choose $u$ uniformly in $[U,2U]$. Its expected cost satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E\log(2\lfloor u\rfloor)\le
 B:=\log64+(\log2)\sum_{j\ge1}r_j<\infty .
\]
At each step independently draw $u$ and choose the outer pattern $D$ to be $D_u$ or its transpose with equal probability, independently of $u$. Put $P_0=[0,1]^2$, $P_{i+1}=D\star P_i$ and $L_i(t)=|\pi_t(P_i)|$. For $0<t<1$ write
\[
 j(t)=\lceil\log_2(1/\tau(t))\rceil,\qquad
 w(t)=r_{j(t)}\ge b(\tau(t)),\qquad w(0)=w(1)=0 .
\]
On the event $J\ge j(t)$, the chosen range of scales satisfies $U\tau(t)\ge16$. For $t\le1/2$ apply Lemma~\ref{lem:shell} in the original orientation. For $t\ge1/2$ use the transposed outer pattern. Transposition exchanges the components of the displacement vector, giving $d_{u,h}(1-t)$, and both inner copies still have section $F_t=\pi_t(P_i)$. All other choices can only decrease section length by \eqref{eq:insert}. With $c=1/128$ this gives
\[
 \mathbb E[L_{i+1}(t)\mid P_i]\le L_i(t)-c\,w(t)L_i(t)^2.
\]
For $x_i=\mathbb E L_i(t)$, Jensen's inequality yields
$x_i-x_{i+1}\ge c\,w(t)x_i^2$. Since $0<x_{i+1}\le x_i$ and $x_0=1$, summing $1/x_{i+1}-1/x_i\ge c\,w(t)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pointwise-mean}
 \mathbb E L_k(t)\le \frac{1}{1+ckw(t)},\qquad
 \mathbb E\log N(P_k)\le Bk .
\end{equation}
The second estimate uses the fact that square counts multiply under insertion.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:small}
For each $0<\varepsilon<1$ there is a pattern $P$, built from shuffles
and their transposes, with $\A(P)\le\varepsilon$ and
$\log N(P)\le C/\varepsilon$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Integrating \eqref{eq:pointwise-mean} gives
$\mathbb E\A(P_k)\le C_0/k$, since $\int_0^1dt/w(t)\le2\sum_{j\ge2}2^{-j}(1+j)^2<\infty$. This is the reason for the floor $(1+j)^{-2}$ in $r_j$.
By Markov's inequality the events $\A(P_k)>4C_0/k$ and
$\log N(P_k)>4Bk$ each have probability at most $1/4$.
Some realization satisfies both bounds. Take $k=\lceil4C_0/\varepsilon\rceil$.
\end{proof}

Every pattern carries a measure with uniform marginals. Take the pushforward of Lebesgue measure by $x\mapsto(x,f(x))$, where $f$ translates each $I_i$ onto $J_i$ up to endpoints. Thickening each of its $N$ squares adds at most $2\delta$ to its section length at any height. Lemma~\ref{lem:hall} now gives
\[
 \alpha_n\le \A(P_{1/n})\le\A(P)+\frac{2N(P)}n.
\]
Take $\varepsilon=2C/\log n$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:small} for large $n$. Then $N(P)\le\sqrt n$, giving $\alpha_n\le C/\log n$ after increasing the constant for small $n$. Fixing $b(r)=r$ makes $C$ absolute. Since $\beta_n\le\alpha_n$, this completes Theorem~\ref{thm:finite}.

\section{Control in every noncoordinate direction}\label{sec:directional}

To realize the mean bound in \eqref{eq:pointwise-mean} at every height
with a single pattern, put independent realizations in different
equal-sized squares. The section length of the resulting pattern is
bounded by the average of their section lengths, whatever the
translations between the copies.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:directional}
For each integer $k\ge1$ there is a pattern $U_k$ obtained by elementary
shuffles and their transposes, whose choice may vary from parent to parent,
such that
\[
 \log N(U_k)\le C k,\qquad
 \sup_{0\le t\le1} w(t)\,|\pi_t(U_k)|\le C/k .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $V$ have the law of $P_k$ conditioned on $N(P_k)\le e^{4Bk}$.
By \eqref{eq:pointwise-mean} and Markov's inequality the conditioning
event has probability at least $3/4$, so for every $t$,
\[
 0\le w(t)|\pi_t(V)|\le 1,\qquad
 \mathbb E\big[w(t)|\pi_t(V)|\big]\le 4/(3ck).
\]
Divide each half of $[0,1]$ into $\lceil k e^{4Bk}\rceil$ equal
intervals, giving a mesh of $q=2\lceil k e^{4Bk}\rceil+1$ points.
Take an even integer $M$ with $2qk^2\le M\le2qk^2+8$ and $M\ge8$.
Draw $M$ independent copies $V_1,\ldots,V_M$ of $V$, and put
$H(t)=M^{-1}\sum_{a=1}^M |\pi_t(V_a)|$. At any mesh point $s$, the
variance of $w(s)H(s)$ is at most $1/M$. Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{w(s)H(s)>(4/(3c)+1)/k\}\le k^2/M .
\]
The sum over the mesh is at most $1/2$. Fix a realization satisfying
the bound at all mesh points.

Each endpoint of a projected interval moves by at most $|t-s|$ between
heights $s$ and $t$. Summing the lengths of the symmetric differences of
one pattern's intervals shows that each $|\pi_t(V_a)|$, and hence $H(t)$,
is $2e^{4Bk}$-Lipschitz in $t$. Choose a mesh point $s$ adjacent to $t$
in the direction of $1/2$. Then $w(t)\le w(s)\le1$ and
$|t-s|\le(2ke^{4Bk})^{-1}$, so $w(t)H(t)\le (4/(3c)+2)/k$.

The shuffle $D_{M/2}$ has $M$ squares, each of side $1/M$. Insert
$V_a$ into its $a$th square. The resulting pattern $U_k$ has
$|\pi_t(U_k)|\le H(t)$ by subadditivity and scaling. Its square count is
at most $M e^{4Bk}$, whose logarithm is $O(k)$.
\end{proof}

\section{A compact set of length one}\label{sec:compact}

We use the same reflection for the area and direction estimates. For any pattern $V$, write $V^*=V\star RV$. In the first quadrant its projections are bounded by those of $V$, and in the second quadrant by those of $RV$, by \eqref{eq:insert}. Thus $\Fav(V^*)\le4\sqrt2\,\A(V)$ by \eqref{eq:angle}. More generally, put $s=|\cos\theta|+|\sin\theta|$ and $t=|\sin\theta|/s$. Up to a translation and a sign, $p_\theta$ on the appropriate factor is $s\pi_t$ on $V$. Hence
\[
 |p_\theta(V^*)|\le s|\pi_t(V)|,\qquad
 \tau(t)=\rho_\theta.
\]

For $j\ge1$ take $Q_j=U_{2^j}^*$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:directional}. The estimate for $w(t)|\pi_t(U_{2^j})|$ gives $\A(U_{2^j})\le C2^{-j}$ by integration. Since $1\le s\le\sqrt2$, the directional estimate and square count give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blocks}
 \Fav(Q_j)\le C2^{-j},\quad
 b(\rho_\theta)|p_\theta(Q_j)|\le C2^{-j},\quad
 \log N(Q_j)\le C2^j ,
\end{equation}
where the middle inequality concerns $\rho_\theta>0$ and uses $w(t)\ge b(\tau(t))$.

The squares of any pattern have total diameter $\sqrt2$. To cover their descendants with total diameter tending to one, use a cyclic half-shift. For even $m$, let $B_m$ match interval $i$ to $i+m/2\pmod m$, numbering from zero. Its squares lie in the disk $E_m$ centered at $(1/2,1/2)$ of diameter
\[
 d_m=\sqrt{1+4/m^2}.
\]
Indeed, the squares in the upper-left quarter are translates of the first along the diagonal. Squared distance to the center is convex along each such translation, so the maximum occurs in the first or last square. These are mirror images across $x+y=1$, and the farthest vertex of the first is $(0,1/2+1/m)$. Central symmetry covers the lower-right quarter.

Start with $T_0=[0,1]^2$, let $T_j=(T_{j-1}\star B_{2j})\star Q_j$ and put $S=\bigcap_{j\ge1} T_j$. These patterns are nested. By \eqref{eq:insert} and \eqref{eq:blocks},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:levels}
 \Fav(T_j)\le C2^{-j},\qquad
 \log N(T_j)\le\sum_{i=1}^j\bigl(\log(2i)+C2^i\bigr)\le D2^j .
\end{equation}
Here $D$ is a constant for the chosen $b$. The largest square side in $T_j$ is at most $2^{-j}$. Inside a square of side $w$ at this level, the next half-shift places all descendants in a disk of diameter $w d_{2j+2}$. These disks cover $S$ and their diameters sum to $d_{2j+2}\to1$. Thus $\mathcal H^1(S)\le1$.

Both coordinate projections of $S$ are $[0,1]$, by compactness of the nested fibers. Projection is $1$-Lipschitz, so $\mathcal H^1(S)\ge1$. In fact, for either coordinate projection $p$ and any Borel $A\subset[0,1]$, the set $S\cap p^{-1}(A)$ projects onto $A$. Thus $p_\#\mu\ge\mathcal L^1$, and the two measures have the same total mass. In either coordinate, the fiber over every point outside the countable set of subdivision endpoints is a singleton. That coordinate selects a unique nested chain of squares whose diameters tend to zero.

To pass from \eqref{eq:levels} to all radii, observe that thickening a square adds $2\delta(|\cos\theta|+|\sin\theta|)$ to its projection length. Hence
\[
 \Fav(S_\delta)\le C2^{-j}+8\delta\exp(D2^j).
\]
In a single direction the length added per square is at most $2\sqrt2\,\delta$. Since $S\subset T_j$ and $T_j$ is an insertion of $Q_j$, we likewise have
\[
  b(\rho_\theta)|p_\theta(S_\delta)|
 \le C2^{-j}+2\sqrt2\,\delta\exp(D2^j)
 \quad(\rho_\theta>0).
\]
For sufficiently small $\delta$ choose the largest $j$ with $D2^j\le\tfrac12\log(1/\delta)$. Then $2^j\asymp\log(1/\delta)$ and the second term is at most $8\sqrt\delta$. Both upper bounds follow, including \eqref{eq:directional-thm}. Boundedness of $S_\delta$ covers the remaining radii below $1/2$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:hall} gives the lower bound with $n=\lceil1/\delta\rceil$. For fixed $\rho_\theta>0$, letting $\delta\downarrow0$ gives $|p_\theta(S)|=0$. Since $\Fav(S)=0$ and $\mathcal H^1(S)=1$, the Besicovitch projection theorem also gives pure unrectifiability \cite[\S1.1]{CSS}.

\subsection*{Length in a ball}

The upper estimate $\mu(B(z,r))\le 2r$ follows from either marginal. A ball can be much smaller than a parent square and much larger than any of its children. To bound its mass from below, we use the order of the children within each elementary subdivision.

Consider one elementary subdivision of a parent square of side $L$. For a shuffle $D_u$ put $h=L/(2u)$, and for a half-shift $B_m$ put $h=L/m$. In both cases the children split into two ordered families. The child sides lie in $[h,2h]$ and sum to $L/2$ in each family. Consecutive centers in a family are at most $5h$ apart.

For $D_u$ these are the two colors. The horizontal and vertical gaps between consecutive centers are at most $4h$ and $2h$, respectively, by the two orders defining the shuffle. For $B_m$ the two families are the halves of the permutation, with consecutive centers $\sqrt2\,h$ apart. In $U_k$ different children can receive different patterns. Each parent is nevertheless subdivided by a single shuffle, possibly transposed. Reflecting a block reflects the whole subdivision inside each parent. All these symmetries preserve sides and center distances, so the bounds apply along every branch.

A square of side $v$ at any elementary level has $\mu$-mass $v$. Indeed, its horizontal interval has length $v$, all points of $S$ above the interior belong to this square, and the two endpoints have zero marginal mass.

Fix $z\in S$ and $0<r\le\diam S$. Follow a nested chain containing $z$ to the first square of side at most $r/8$. Its parent has side $L>r/8$, and $h\le r/8$. The family containing this child has at least $L/(4h)>r/(32h)$ members. Choose $1+\lfloor r/(32h)\rfloor$ consecutive members including the child. Each chosen center is within $5r/32$ of the child's center. Each square has circumradius at most $\sqrt2\,h$, so all the chosen squares lie in $B(z,r)$, since $5r/32+2\sqrt2\,h<r$. Their masses add, giving
\[
 \mu(B(z,r))\ge h\bigl(1+\lfloor r/(32h)\rfloor\bigr)\ge r/32 .
\]
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:compact}.

\begin{corollary}[Directional bounds for permutations]\label{cor:simultaneous}
Let $b:(0,1/2]\to(0,1]$ be nondecreasing. Condition~\eqref{eq:criterion} is necessary and sufficient for the following property. For each $n\ge2$ one can choose a single permutation $\sigma_n$ such that
\[
 \Fav(P_{\sigma_n})\le\frac{C_b}{\log n},\qquad
 b(\tau(t))\,|\pi_t(P_{\sigma_n})|\le
 \frac{C_b}{\log n}\quad(0<t<1).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For sufficiency select $P_{\sigma_n}\subset S_{1/n}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:hall} and use Theorem~\ref{thm:compact}, increasing $C_b$ for $n=2$. Necessity follows from \eqref{eq:reciprocal}, as shown in Section~\ref{sec:entropy}.
\end{proof}

\smallskip
The finite and compact upper bounds also recover one another at the level of Favard decay. Lemma~\ref{lem:hall} gives the finite bound from the compact one at $\delta=1/n$. Conversely, Theorem~\ref{thm:finite} alone supplies equal-grid patterns at $n_j=\lceil\exp(C_0 2^j)\rceil$ with $\A(P_j)\le 2^{-j}/(4\sqrt2)$ for sufficiently large $C_0$. Reflection, insertion and the half-shifts then give \eqref{eq:levels}, length one and the uniform marginals. The local mass estimate uses the order of the children in the shuffle, and the uniform oblique projection estimate uses Lemma~\ref{lem:directional}. The estimates leave open the optimal leading constants and the existence of $\lim_{n\to\infty}(\log n)\alpha_n$. Monotonicity in $n$ is a separate question in both formulations \cite[pp.~28--29]{Letter}, \cite[Conjecture~3]{Kuklinski}.
