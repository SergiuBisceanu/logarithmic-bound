\begin{abstract}
Match the $n$ equal intervals on the bottom of the unit square to those on the top by a permutation. The least area of the union of the resulting parallelograms is of order $1/\log n$, as conjectured by Kaczynski. The same construction gives a compact $1$-Ahlfors regular set of Hausdorff length one whose length measure projects to Lebesgue measure on both coordinate axes and whose $\delta$-neighborhood has Favard length of order $1/\log(1/\delta)$.
\end{abstract}

Divide the top and bottom edges of the unit square into $n$ equal intervals. Match the intervals by a permutation and join each matched pair by a parallelogram. Their areas add to one. How small can the area of their union be? Write $\alpha_n$ for the minimum. Kuklinski proved that $\alpha_n\le C(\log n)^{-p}$ for some $p>0$ and recorded the conjecture that the exponent is one \cite[Conjecture~2 and Theorem~4]{Kuklinski}.

\begin{theorem}[Kaczynski's second conjecture]\label{thm:finite}
For every $n\ge2$,
\[
  \frac1{H_n}\le\alpha_n\le\frac C{\log n},\qquad
  H_n=\sum_{j=1}^n\frac1j,
\]
where $C$ is absolute.
\end{theorem}

The two endpoints of a segment give a point $(x,y)$ in a second copy of the unit square. At height $t$ the segment has horizontal coordinate $\pi_t(x,y)=(1-t)x+ty$. Thus the strip joining $I$ to $J$ is encoded by the square $I\times J$, and its section at height $t$ is the interval $\pi_t(I\times J)$. This description also gives a compact version of the problem.

For compact $K\subset\R^2$, put $K_\delta=K+[-\delta,\delta]^2$ and
\[
  \Fav(K)=\int_0^\pi |p_\theta(K)|\,d\theta,
  \qquad p_\theta(x,y)=x\cos\theta+y\sin\theta.
\]
Write $p_x,p_y$ for the coordinate projections and $\mathcal L^1$ for Lebesgue measure on $[0,1]$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:compact}
There is a compact set $S\subset[0,1]^2$ of Hausdorff length one such that, for $\mu=\mathcal H^1|_S$,
\[
 (p_x)_\#\mu=(p_y)_\#\mu=\mathcal L^1,\qquad
 \mu(B(z,r))\asymp r\quad(z\in S,\ 0<r\le\diam S).
\]
Moreover,
\[
  \Fav(S_\delta)\asymp\frac1{\log(1/\delta)}
  \qquad (0<\delta<\tfrac12).
\]
The comparison constants are absolute.
\end{theorem}

Mattila's projection estimate gives the logarithmic lower bound for sets of positive one-dimensional Hausdorff content \cite{CSS,Mattila}. Chang, Shmerkin and Suomala constructed $1$-Ahlfors regular random Cantor sets with this sharp Favard decay \cite{CSS}. Cs\"ornyei and Suomala characterized the measures that can occur as one projection of a purely unrectifiable length measure \cite{CS}. Here both coordinate projections are prescribed, and either one already accounts for the entire length of $S$. The choice of scales matters. On a fixed $L$-adic grid, nested patterns with one square per column at every level have Favard length at least $c_L(\log j)/j$ at level $j$ \cite[Theorem~A.1]{CSS}.

The construction uses two ordered families of copies of the same pattern. At a fixed height, the sections of a matched red and blue copy differ by a translation. Averaging over the scale of the copies turns the overlap into a quadratic saving in area, at bounded average cost in the logarithm of the number of squares.

\section{Two estimates on the equal grid}

For a compact set $K$ of endpoints, let $\A(K)=\int_0^1|\pi_t(K)|\,dt$. For a union of endpoint squares this is the area covered by the strips. Reflection $R(x,y)=(x,1-y)$ interchanges the two quadrants of projection directions. Indeed, setting $t=\sin\theta/(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:angle}
 \A(K)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{|p_\theta(K)|}{(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)^3}\,d\theta,
 \qquad
 \A(K)+\A(RK)\le\Fav(K)\le 2\sqrt2\,\bigl(\A(K)+\A(RK)\bigr).
\end{equation}

Let $P_\sigma$ be the union of the $n$ closed grid squares selected by a permutation $\sigma$. Let $Z$ count its strips through a point and put $E=\int Z^2$. Since $\int Z=1$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\A(P_\sigma)\ge1/E$. Only inversions contribute to the pairwise overlaps. If an inverted pair has index gaps $p$ and $q$ on the two edges, its intersection area is
\[
 \frac1n\int_0^1\bigl(1-|p-(p+q)t|\bigr)_+\,dt
 =\frac1{n(p+q)}\le\frac1{4n}\left(\frac1p+\frac1q\right).
\]
The integrand is a tent of height one and base $2/(p+q)$, supported in $[0,1]$.

Let $E'$ be the second moment for the reflected pattern $RP_\sigma$. Every pair $i<j$ crosses in exactly one of the two matchings, with the same unsigned gaps $p_{ij}=j-i$ and $q_{ij}=|\sigma(j)-\sigma(i)|$. On each edge precisely $n-d$ pairs have gap $d$. Counting each intersection twice gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:harmonic}
 E+E'=2+\frac2n\sum_{i<j}\frac1{p_{ij}+q_{ij}}
 \le 2+\frac1n\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}\frac{n-d}{d}=H_n+1.
\end{equation}
The diagonal terms alone give $E'\ge1$, so $E\le H_n$. This proves $\alpha_n\ge1/H_n$. Using both quadrants in \eqref{eq:angle} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:both}
 \Fav(P_\sigma)\ge\frac1E+\frac1{E'}\ge\frac4{E+E'}\ge\frac4{H_n+1}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Grid matching]\label{lem:hall}
If a compact $K\subset[0,1]^2$ carries a probability measure with uniform coordinate marginals, then for each $n\ge2$ some $P_\sigma$ lies in $K_{1/n}$. In particular,
\[
 \alpha_n\le\A(K_{1/n}),\qquad \Fav(K_{1/n})\ge\frac4{H_n+1}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use half-open grid intervals $I_i$, closing the last one at $1$. For the given measure $\nu$, the matrix $a_{ij}=n\nu(I_i\times I_j)$ has all row and column sums equal to one. Any $k$ rows therefore have positive entries in at least $k$ columns. Hall's theorem \cite{Hall} supplies a permutation of positive entries. Each selected cell meets $K$, so its closure lies in $K_{1/n}$. Apply \eqref{eq:both} and the definition of $\alpha_n$.
\end{proof}

\section{The shuffle}

A \emph{pattern} is a finite union $P=\bigcup_i(I_i\times J_i)$ in which each family of intervals partitions $[0,1]$ up to endpoints and $|I_i|=|J_i|=w_i>0$. Write $N(P)$ for the number of squares. If $P=\bigcup_i(v_i+w_i[0,1]^2)$, insert a copy of $Q$ in each square by setting $P\star Q=\bigcup_i(v_i+w_iQ)$. For any linear projection $\pi$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:insert}
 |\pi(P\star Q)|\le\min\{|\pi(P)|,|\pi(Q)|\}.
\end{equation}
Containment gives the first bound. The second follows by summing the copy ratios.

Fix a real $u\ge4$. Partition $[0,u]$ into $m=\lfloor u\rfloor$ pieces, the first $m-1$ of length one and the last of length $1+u-m$. Make a red and a blue copy of the partition. On two edges of length $2u$, arrange the pieces in the orders
\[
 \begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
 \text{bottom}&R_1,B_1,R_2,B_2,\ldots,R_m,B_m,\\
 \text{top}&B_1,B_2,\ldots,B_m,R_1,R_2,\ldots,R_m .
 \end{array}
\]
Match equal labels and rescale by $1/(2u)$ to obtain $D_u$. Its sides lie in $[1/(2u),1/u]$. Put $P'=D_u\star P$, $F_t=\pi_t(P)$ and $\ell(t)=|F_t|$. Within each color the squares occur in the same order on both axes. Their projections have disjoint interiors and total covered length $\ell(t)/2$ after insertion.

Pair $R_i$ with $B_{i+1}$ for $1\le i\le m-2$. In units of their common width, blue starts three units to the right of red on the bottom and $u-1$ to its left on the top. Its displacement at height $t$ is
\[
 d_u(t)=3(1-t)-(u-1)t=3-(u+2)t.
\]
The paired red copies have total width $(m-2)/(2u)\ge1/8$. Adding their disjoint overlaps gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:save}
 \ell(t)-|\pi_t(P')|\ge\tfrac18\,|F_t\cap(F_t+d_u(t))|.
\end{equation}

Choose $u$ on $[4,\infty)$ with probability density $u^{-3/2}$. Fix $0<t\le1/2$. To obtain a displacement $-s$ with $0\le s\le1$, take $u=(3-2t+s)/t$. Pairing $R_i$ with $B_{i+1}$ ensures that even the smallest of these parameters, $3/t-2$, is at least four. In this range
\[
 u^{-3/2}\,du=\frac{\sqrt t}{(3-2t+s)^{3/2}}\,ds\ge\frac{\sqrt t}{8}\,ds.
\]
For any measurable $F\subset[0,1]$,
$\int_0^1 |F\cap(F-s)|\,ds=|F|^2/2$ by Fubini, counting the half of $F\times F$ above the diagonal. Consequently \eqref{eq:save} gives an expected saving of at least $c\sqrt t\,\ell(t)^2$ at height $t$. Transposing $P$ exchanges $t$ and $1-t$. We may therefore start the shuffle with the orientation for which $\int_0^{1/2}\ell(t)\,dt\ge\A(P)/2$. Since $\int_0^{1/2}t^{-1/2}\,dt=\sqrt2$, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gain}
 \mathbb E\,[\A(P)-\A(P')]\ge c\int_0^{1/2}\sqrt t\,\ell(t)^2\,dt
 \ge c'\A(P)^2 .
\end{equation}
Small heights require $u$ of order $1/t$. The density supplies these scales at bounded average cost, since $\mathbb E\log(2\lfloor u\rfloor)\le\log 8+2$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:small}
For $0<\varepsilon<1$, a finite sequence of shuffles and transpositions produces a pattern $P$ with $\A(P)\le\varepsilon$ and $\log N(P)\le C/\varepsilon$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $a=\A(P)$, $a'=\A(P')$ and $M=2\lfloor u\rfloor$. By \eqref{eq:gain}, $C(a-a')/a^2-(1+\log M)$ has positive expectation for a sufficiently large absolute $C$. Choose a $u$ for which it is positive. Whenever $a>\varepsilon$ this gives
\[
 1+\log M\le 4C\left(\frac1{a'+\varepsilon}-\frac1{a+\varepsilon}\right),
\]
since $a'\le a$ and $(a'+\varepsilon)(a+\varepsilon)\le4a^2$. Start with the unit square and continue until the area is at most $\varepsilon$. After any $k$ steps taken under this rule, the pattern $P_k$ satisfies
\[
 k+\log N(P_k)\le 4C\left(\frac1{\A(P_k)+\varepsilon}-\frac1{1+\varepsilon}\right)
 <\frac{4C}{\varepsilon}.
\]
Here the copy counts multiply and the reciprocals telescope. The bound on $k$ forces termination, and the same inequality bounds $\log N(P_k)$ at the stopping step.
\end{proof}

Every pattern carries a measure with uniform marginals. Take the pushforward of Lebesgue measure by $x\mapsto(x,f(x))$, where $f$ translates each $I_i$ onto $J_i$ up to endpoints. Thickening each of its $N$ squares adds at most $2\delta$ to its section length at any height. Lemma~\ref{lem:hall} now gives
\[
 \alpha_n\le \A(P_{1/n})\le\A(P)+\frac{2N(P)}n.
\]
Take $\varepsilon=2C/\log n$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:small} for large $n$. Then $N(P)\le\sqrt n$, proving Theorem~\ref{thm:finite} after increasing the constant for small $n$.

\section{A compact set of length one}

By \eqref{eq:insert}, $P\star RP$ has first-quadrant projections bounded by those of $P$ and second-quadrant projections bounded by those of $RP$. Equations \eqref{eq:angle} and \eqref{eq:insert} give $\Fav(P\star RP)\le4\sqrt2\,\A(P)$. Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:small} with $\varepsilon=2^{-j}/(4\sqrt2)$ and inserting the reflected copy gives patterns $Q_j$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blocks}
 \Fav(Q_j)\le 2^{-j},\qquad \log N(Q_j)\le C2^j .
\end{equation}

The squares of any pattern have total diameter $\sqrt2$. To cover their descendants with total diameter tending to one, use a cyclic half-shift. For even $m$, let $B_m$ match interval $i$ to $i+m/2\pmod m$, numbering from zero. Its squares lie in the disk $D_m$ centered at $(1/2,1/2)$ of diameter
\[
 d_m=\sqrt{1+4/m^2}.
\]
Indeed, the squares in the upper-left quarter are translates of the first along the diagonal. Squared distance to the center is convex along each such translation, so the maximum occurs in the first or last square. These are mirror images across $x+y=1$, and the farthest vertex of the first is $(0,1/2+1/m)$. Central symmetry covers the lower-right quarter.

Start with $T_0=[0,1]^2$, let $T_j=(T_{j-1}\star B_{2j})\star Q_j$ and put $S=\bigcap_{j\ge1} T_j$. These patterns are nested. By \eqref{eq:insert} and \eqref{eq:blocks},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:levels}
 \Fav(T_j)\le2^{-j},\qquad
 \log N(T_j)\le\sum_{i=1}^j\bigl(\log(2i)+C2^i\bigr)\le b2^j .
\end{equation}
The largest square side in $T_j$ is at most $2^{-j}$. Inside a square of side $w$ at this level, the next half-shift places all descendants in a disk of diameter $w d_{2j+2}$. These disks cover $S$ and their diameters sum to $d_{2j+2}\to1$. Thus $\mathcal H^1(S)\le1$.

Both coordinate projections of $S$ are $[0,1]$, by compactness of the nested fibers. Projection is $1$-Lipschitz, so $\mathcal H^1(S)\ge1$. In fact, for either coordinate projection $p$ and any Borel $A\subset[0,1]$, the set $S\cap p^{-1}(A)$ projects onto $A$. Thus $p_\#\mu\ge\mathcal L^1$, and the two measures have the same total mass. In either coordinate, the fiber over every point outside the countable set of subdivision endpoints is a singleton. That coordinate selects a unique nested chain of squares whose diameters tend to zero.

To pass from \eqref{eq:levels} to all radii, observe that thickening a square adds $2\delta(|\cos\theta|+|\sin\theta|)$ to its projection length. Hence
\[
 \Fav(S_\delta)\le 2^{-j}+8\delta\exp(b2^j).
\]
For sufficiently small $\delta$ choose the largest $j$ with $b2^j\le\tfrac12\log(1/\delta)$. Then $2^j\asymp\log(1/\delta)$ and the second term is at most $8\sqrt\delta$. The remaining radii below $1/2$ are covered by increasing the constant. For the lower bound apply Lemma~\ref{lem:hall} with $n=\lceil1/\delta\rceil$. In particular $\Fav(S)=0$, so the Besicovitch projection theorem gives pure unrectifiability \cite[\S1.1]{CSS}.

\section{Length in a ball}

The upper estimate $\mu(B(z,r))\le 2r$ follows from either marginal. A ball can be much smaller than a parent square and much larger than any of its children. To bound its mass from below, we use the order of the children within each elementary subdivision.

Consider one elementary subdivision of a parent square of side $L$. For a shuffle $D_u$ put $h=L/(2u)$, and for a half-shift $B_m$ put $h=L/m$. In both cases the children split into two ordered families. The child sides lie in $[h,2h]$ and sum to $L/2$ in each family. Consecutive centers in a family are at most $5h$ apart.

For $D_u$ these are the two colors. The horizontal and vertical gaps between consecutive centers are at most $4h$ and $2h$, respectively, by the two orders defining the shuffle. For $B_m$ the two families are the halves of the permutation, with consecutive centers $\sqrt2\,h$ apart. Each $Q_j$ is built from elementary shuffles and their images under symmetries of the square, since a reflection or transposition acts on both factors of an insertion. These symmetries preserve sides and center distances.

A square of side $v$ at any elementary level has $\mu$-mass $v$. Indeed, its horizontal interval has length $v$, all points of $S$ above the interior belong to this square, and the two endpoints have zero marginal mass.

Fix $z\in S$ and $0<r\le\diam S$. Follow a nested chain containing $z$ to the first square of side at most $r/8$. Its parent has side $L>r/8$, and $h\le r/8$. The family containing this child has at least $L/(4h)>r/(32h)$ members. Choose $1+\lfloor r/(32h)\rfloor$ consecutive members including the child. Each chosen center is within $5r/32$ of the child's center. Each square has circumradius at most $\sqrt2\,h$, so all the chosen squares lie in $B(z,r)$, since $5r/32+2\sqrt2\,h<r$. Their masses add, giving
\[
 \mu(B(z,r))\ge h\bigl(1+\lfloor r/(32h)\rfloor\bigr)\ge r/32 .
\]
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:compact}.

\smallskip
The finite and compact upper bounds also recover one another at the level of Favard decay. Lemma~\ref{lem:hall} gives the finite bound from the compact one at $\delta=1/n$. Conversely, Theorem~\ref{thm:finite} alone supplies equal-grid patterns at $n_j=\lceil\exp(C_0 2^j)\rceil$ with $\A(P_j)\le 2^{-j}/(4\sqrt2)$ for sufficiently large $C_0$. Reflection, insertion and the half-shifts then give \eqref{eq:levels}, length one and the uniform marginals. The local lower mass bound uses the order of the children in the shuffle.
